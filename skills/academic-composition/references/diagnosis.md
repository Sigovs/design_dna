# Diagnosis

*Read when: critiquing an existing design, or when a client complaint has to be
converted into a decision. Supports the critique role in
[TASTE.md §2b](../../../TASTE.md#2b-the-critique-panel).*

---

## Every diagnosis has five parts

A critique without all five is an opinion:

1. **What is observed** — in the artefact, not in the feeling. "Five masses of
   equal value across the first two screens", not "it feels flat".
2. **Why it affects the total composition** — the consequence, stated as a
   relationship that fails.
3. **The level it lives at** — content, typography, imagery, colour, composition,
   or interaction. Fixing at the wrong level is the expensive error.
4. **The smallest structural intervention likely to improve it** — the cheapest
   change that addresses the cause, not the symptom.
5. **What must not change** — what is working and is at risk from the fix. Without
   this, a critique invites a redesign of things that were right.

## Locating the level first

| The complaint | Often actually |
|---|---|
| "The colours feel wrong" | Tonal structure — the values are equal and hue is carrying hierarchy |
| "It needs more whitespace" | Interval hierarchy — every gap is the same, not too few gaps |
| "It looks cluttered" | Surface fragmentation, or too many centres |
| "It's boring" | Meter without rhythm, or symmetry with uniform value |
| "The hierarchy is unclear" | Content has no hierarchy to express |
| "It feels cheap" | Detail density and edge quality, or crop-through-joints in imagery |
| "It doesn't feel premium" | Unusable as stated — demand the observable version |
| "It's too busy on mobile" | Recomposition never happened |
| "The CTA underperforms" | No direction converges on the centre of action |

**Do not recommend a new accent colour because a page feels empty**, and do not
recommend more whitespace because it feels cramped. Both are level errors.

## The vocabulary

Use these; they are precise, and each implies its own fix.

**Mass and hierarchy**
- collapsed mass hierarchy · excessive equal weight · disconnected major masses ·
  component-first composition · functional grouping without compositional unity

**Centre**
- weak compositional centre · competing secondary centres · accidental centre
  drift · device outranking subject ([C13](../SKILL.md#invariant))

**Figure-ground and space**
- unstable figure-ground · trapped negative space · unresolved negative space ·
  excessive surface fragmentation · atmospheric flattening

**Balance and tension**
- weak counterweight · arbitrary asymmetry · excessive centralisation ·
  unresolved visual tension · unintended edge tension · tangent collision

**Direction**
- broken directional flow · image-to-layout directional conflict · centrifugal
  opening · no convergence on the centre of action

**Rhythm**
- repetitive sectional meter · rhythm without variation · absent pause ·
  premature culmination · decorative variety without structural development

**Unity**
- autonomous section composition · weak plastic unity · competing compositional
  systems · accidental style shift

**Format and responsive**
- crop-dependent hierarchy · desktop balance collapse on mobile · format-blind
  reuse · first-screen priority undecided

**Content dependency**
- placeholder-dependent mechanism · single-asset composition · long-content
  collapse

## Ranking findings

Order by **how much of the composition depends on the fix**:

1. Failures of mass structure and centre — everything else is built on them.
2. Failures of tonal hierarchy — they invalidate every colour judgement above.
3. Failures of direction and rhythm — they decide whether the page is read.
4. Failures of edge, tangency and detail — real, and cheap to fix last.

A critique that opens with a tangency while the page has no dominant has ranked
itself wrongly.

## Critique discipline

- **Name the evidence.** Position, size, value, relationship. No adjective without
  an observation attached.
- **Separate law from taste.** An invariant breach is a defect; a dialect
  preference is a position, and it must be labelled as one
  ([TASTE.md §1](../../../TASTE.md#1-the-two-tiers)).
- **Do not stack fixes.** One intervention, then look again. Compositions respond
  non-linearly — lowering one mass often resolves three symptoms.
- **Say what is working.** Not for morale: to protect it from the fix.
- **Accept the null result.** "The composition is sound; the problem is the copy"
  is a legitimate and frequently correct finding.
