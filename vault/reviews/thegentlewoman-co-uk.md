### The E3 question — what the frames actually show

Five signals are visible in `hero.jpg` and `mobile.jpg`. They are listed as
observations, not as a rule: one record cannot establish which of them is
load-bearing, and that is exactly why E3 stays a tension.

1. **The subject has edges.** What the emptiness surrounds is a magazine cover —
   a bounded rectangle photographed as an object. Space around a thing that ends
   somewhere reads as framing. Space around a layout reads as unfinished.
2. **Extent is declared: `1 / 8`.** The hero carries pagination at the far right
   of the caption rule. The page states that seven more exist. Emptiness with a
   counter on it is a position in a sequence; emptiness without one is all there
   is.
3. **Exactly one sentence, placed rather than dropped.** The caption sits on a
   hairline with the counter — the composition Alex calls metadata as
   composition. One sentence is not silence; zero sentences would be.
4. **Chrome is present and coloured.** Nav populated at the top, wordmark at the
   right, and the turquoise applied through all of it. A page rendering its
   identity colour consistently cannot be read as one that failed to load —
   which is precisely the misreading recorded at the other end of this axis.
5. **The mobile frame settles what the desktop hides.** `mobile.jpg` is the same
   eight items stacked as a labelled index — Magazine · Club · Library · Shop —
   each with image, category and caption. So the desktop emptiness is a
   **transport decision about one item at a time**, not an absence of content.
   Same content, different carriage.

**What this does not settle**, and Alex named it: whether the boundary is
compositional at all, or also depends on page expectations and information
density. A magazine's landing page is granted patience an inventory page is not.
Nothing here separates those two explanations, and one more record at either end
would.
