# Dialect — retro-futurist

> **Status: library.** An art direction available as an input to a Design Read —
> not a claim about Alex's own taste, and not a house dialect. The only route to
> `confirmed` is the evidence rule in
> [vault/README.md](../vault/README.md#creating-a-new-dialect).

**Core thesis.** Take a specific historical idea of the future and run it on present
technology: borrow the *logic* of a period's optimism or unease, reinterpret it, and
let the gap between what was imagined and what exists carry the meaning.

**Emotional character.** Speculative — either optimistic in a way the present has
lost, or uneasy in a way the period did not intend. A visitor feels a deliberate
displacement in time.

---

## DECISION LOGIC

**Name the period and the source, or do not use this dialect.** *1969 mission
documentation. 1978 Japanese hi-fi. 1984 terminal software. 1992 CD-ROM optimism.*
Precision is what separates the dialect from nostalgia; "retro" without a date is
costume shopping.

**Borrow the logic, not the artefacts.** A period's real contribution is a way of
organising: mission documentation's numbered discipline, hi-fi's panel-and-legend
system, terminal software's monospaced grid. Take that structure and run it on
content it never saw.

**Reinterpret, never re-enact.** The result should be impossible in the period — it
uses the period's reasoning on today's problem, at today's resolution. If it could
pass as an artefact, it is a replica and it says nothing.

**Selective, not total.** One or two period mechanisms, applied consistently. The
rest of the page belongs to the present, which is what makes the borrowed part read
as a choice.

**When two goods conflict, specificity wins over charm.** The exact reference is the
whole value; a general vintage feeling is the cliché.

## SYSTEMS — what this dialect does to each domain

| Domain | This dialect's behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Composition / grid** | Adopts the period's organising logic — panel systems, numbered plates, terminal columns, bordered fields — applied to modern content at modern scale |
| **Hierarchy / density** | Often high density inherited from the source (documentation, instrument panels, catalogues). Rank by label, number and enclosure as much as by size |
| **Typography behaviour** | One or two faces with genuine period lineage — a grotesque of the right decade, a technical mono, a specific display cut — used at contemporary sizes and correctly spaced. Never a distressed novelty font |
| **Spacing / rhythm** | Inherited from the source system: regular, often tight, frequently tabular. Rhythm from repeating the period's module |
| **Colour / contrast logic** | A narrow palette taken from a real artefact — a specific film stock, plastic, print process or phosphor. Two or three colours doing classification work, not atmosphere |
| **Image behaviour** | Period *treatment* on present subjects, consistently: one grade, one halftone, one duotone. Reinterpretation, never a filter stack of several |
| **Containers / borders / geometry** | Frequently the period's own: legends, bezels, plates, rules, boxed fields. Consistent, single-valued, structural |
| **Depth / materiality** | Material of the period — plastic, phosphor, printed ink, brushed panel — read honestly. Depth is shallow and physical, not skeuomorphic mimicry |
| **Motion / interaction** | One mechanism borrowed from period equipment: a stepped counter, a segment reveal, a switch's discrete state. Discrete rather than smooth, and used once |
| **Information presentation** | Its strength: numbered plates, legends, keyed diagrams, index cards, tabulated readouts — the period's information design applied to real current data |

## SIGNATURE DEVICES

- **The keyed plate** — a numbered or lettered legend tying labels to a diagram or
  image. Its job is to make the period's documentation logic do real work.
- **The period module** — the source system's repeating unit (panel, card, bezel,
  column) carried consistently. Its job is to organise, not to decorate.
- **The discrete mechanism** — one stepped, mechanical interaction. Its job is to
  make the interface behave like the period's equipment, exactly once.

As a SIGNATURE influence in another dialect's hybrid, borrow exactly one — most often
the discrete mechanism or the keyed plate.

## WHAT IT AVOIDS

Nostalgia collage (several decades at once); chrome and airbrush without a source;
VHS glitch, scanlines and CRT curvature as effects; grain and dust as filters;
distressed novelty typefaces; "retro-futurism" as a synonym for purple-and-teal
gradients; and any borrowed element that cannot name its artefact.

## FALSE VERSIONS — the clichés that wear its clothes

**Nostalgia collage** is the failure: a chrome wordmark, a sunset gradient, a wireframe
grid, VHS artefacts, a synth-wave palette, and a serif from a different decade — none
of it from a single source, all of it from a reference board. It reproduces a
*mood about the past* rather than the past's reasoning, so it can only ever look like
every other page that used the same board.

Tells: no date can be named; two or more periods are present; the effects are
post-processing rather than structure; and removing the effects leaves an ordinary
modern page underneath — proof that the period never touched the decisions.

Second false version: **the replica** — a faithful reproduction of a period artefact.
Well-made and pointless: it takes no position on the present, and it is also the fast
route to reproducing somebody's protected assets, which §6 (d) forbids.

## FIT

**Good fit:** technology and software with a heritage claim; audio, aerospace,
instrument and gaming culture; archives and anniversaries; cultural programming;
speculative or research projects; audiences who will recognise and enjoy the source.

**Poor fit:** trust-critical services (finance, health, safety) where displacement in
time reads as unreliability; briefs with no plausible relationship to any period;
audiences who will read the reference as dated rather than deliberate. Prefer
`swiss-editorial` or `technical-luxury`.

## PURE MODE

The whole art direction: one named period's organising logic, its type lineage, its
palette from a real artefact, its information design, one discrete mechanism.

**It does not license:** period type at period sizes below the functional floor
([I7](../skills/typography-taste/SKILL.md#invariant)), phosphor-on-black failing AA,
scanline overlays over reading text
([D9](../skills/anti-patterns/SKILL.md#dialect) and
[DM9](../skills/dimensionality/SKILL.md#invariant)), or period interaction that
breaks keyboard operation. The period's ergonomics were worse; only its reasoning is
borrowed.

## HYBRID RELATIONSHIPS

**Useful**
- **+ `swiss-editorial`** — *period logic, disciplined by a grid.* Swiss owns
  structure, hierarchy and density; retro contributes one mechanism and the palette
  logic. The safest home for this dialect, because the grid resists costume.
- **+ `brutalist-utility`** — exposed structure with a period's information design;
  both like visible systems and neither wants softness.
- **+ `expressive-poster`** — a period display cut used for one scale collision.
  Bounded tightly, it is strong; unbounded, it becomes pastiche immediately.

**Dangerous**
- **+ `cinematic-industrial`** — both reach for spectacle and period atmosphere;
  together they produce the synth-wave cliché almost automatically.
- **+ `refined-elegance`** — the measured system has no appetite for the costume, so
  the period reads as an applied decoration on a quiet page.
- **+ `organic-tactile`** — two different materialities, both wanting the image
  treatment; the library splits.

## ACCESSIBILITY AND USABILITY

The period is the risk: real artefacts had low contrast, tiny labels, and interactions
that do not survive a keyboard. Borrowed density must still clear the floor and AA;
one discrete mechanism must have a keyboard equivalent and a reduced-motion still; and
overlays that mimic a display surface must never sit over reading text. Recognition
also varies — if the source is unknown to the audience, the page is simply strange, and
that is a fit question, not a styling one.

## DIAGNOSTICS

1. Name the period, the artefact and the year. Can you?
2. Is one source in play, or several?
3. Is the borrowing structural, or is it post-processing?
4. Remove the period surface. Does the borrowed *logic* remain visible?
5. Could this have existed in the period? If yes, it is a replica.
6. Does the audience know the reference — and does the page work if they do not?

## THE LOGIC, COMPACTLY

*Brief: a company restoring and selling 1970s hi-fi separates, 30 units in stock,
detailed condition reports, buyers who are collectors.*

The source is named precisely: late-70s Japanese hi-fi manuals — panel-and-legend
layouts, keyed diagrams, tabulated specification, two-colour print. That logic runs on
present content: each unit gets a keyed plate where numbered callouts tie condition
notes to the actual photograph, which is what a manual did and what a modern listing
usually fails to do. The palette comes from one real artefact — the brushed panel, the
warm off-white of the printed manual, one signal orange — so it is specific rather than
tasteful. Type has period lineage but sits at contemporary sizes with correct tracking,
because the manual's 7pt legend is not a floor to imitate. One discrete mechanism: the
input selector steps between units with a stepped counter, keyboard-operable, with a
still for reduced motion. Nothing is distressed, no grain, no CRT. What the dialect
decided was not "make it look vintage" — it was that *the manual's information design
is better than a modern listing's*, and that borrowing it says something true about the
seller.
