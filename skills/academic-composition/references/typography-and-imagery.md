# Typography and imagery as compositional forces

*Read when: art-directing a hero, setting a headline that carries a composition,
or diagnosing a page where the type and the image fight. Supports
[C6](../SKILL.md#invariant), [C3](../SKILL.md#invariant) and
[C13](../SKILL.md#invariant).*

This file is about type and image **as masses and forces**. Setting belongs to
[typography-taste](../../typography-taste/SKILL.md); chromatic relationships
belong to [color-taste](../../color-taste/SKILL.md).

---

## Type is a mass before it is content

Every text element is a tonal shape:

- **A headline is an object.** Its silhouette — set by the line breaks — is part of
  the composition. Two lines and three lines are different masses, and a headline
  that reflows from three to two at a breakpoint has changed the composition, not
  just the layout.
- **Body copy is texture.** Its value is set by size, weight, leading and measure.
  Tight leading darkens the block; open leading lightens it. That is a tonal
  decision made in a type panel.
- **Metadata is rhythm.** Small repeated labels, dates, specs and numbers form a
  regular beat under the larger masses.
- **Display type at scale is an image.** Past a certain size a word stops being read
  first and starts being seen — it becomes a field, an edge, and a direction.

**Line breaks are compositional decisions** ([typography I3](../../typography-taste/SKILL.md#invariant)).
A break can change the headline's silhouette, its optical centre, its balance
against an adjacent image, and where the eye exits. Do not leave the breaks of a
composition-carrying headline to the browser at the widths you care about.

Check the composition with **real text at real lengths** — the longest and the
shortest case. A mass scheme validated on lorem is validated on a fiction.

## Type against image

Two strong things in one frame need a decided relationship. Four legitimate ones:

1. **Continuity** — the type follows the image's forces: aligned to its horizon,
   set into the direction of travel, sitting in the quiet region the image
   already has.
2. **Counterpoint** — the type opposes them deliberately: horizontal calm across a
   diagonal, a hard edge against an atmospheric field.
3. **Framing** — the type neutralises the image, containing it and ranking it as
   support.
4. **Interruption** — the type cuts the image, decisively. Committed overlap, not
   a near-miss.

What is not legitimate: both at full force with no relationship stated. That is
the "hero image and huge headline fighting" page, where each element is
individually good.

## Text over imagery

Legibility is fixed at the **background** layer, against the worst-case region the
text actually overlaps — that is [color I4](../../color-taste/SKILL.md#invariant),
and it binds.

Compositionally, a full-width dark gradient is only one answer, and it is the one
that erodes the image. Consider first:

- **Art-directed crop** — choose or crop a frame with a quiet region where the text
  belongs.
- **Relocation** — put the text where the image is already quiet.
- **A separate field** — text beside the image rather than on it. Often stronger,
  and it keeps the photograph whole.
- **A local plate** — a contained solid behind the text block, committed as a form.
- **Grading** — darken or lift the image as a decision about the image.
- **Directional scrim** — a gradient that follows the image's own structure.
- **Selective blur** — used sparingly, as a spatial statement.
- **A different image**, or a different composition on mobile.

## Imagery as internal composition

Read every image as a composition before placing it:

- **Subject position** and how much air it has.
- **Direction** — gaze, travel, perspective, lean
  ([direction-and-movement](direction-and-movement.md)).
- **Horizon height** — sets stability.
- **Tonal masses** — where the image's own darks and lights sit, because they join
  the page's tonal structure.
- **Internal negative space** — often the best place for type.
- **Detail density** — a busy image cannot serve as a quiet field, whatever its
  size.
- **Crop tolerance** — how much can be cut before the subject is damaged.
- **Edge activity** — what is happening at the image's own boundaries.

**Reject an image that works alone and damages the page.** The commonest case: a
beautiful frame whose direction points off the page, chosen on its own merits and
placed without regard to what it does to the reading.

Across multiple images, check **continuity**: light direction, distance, tonal
range and treatment. Six images from six shoots read as six decisions and destroy
unity ([unity-and-plastic-connection](unity-and-plastic-connection.md)).

## The device-over-subject failure

[C13](../SKILL.md#invariant) is where type and image most often go wrong together:
the type treatment becomes the subject and the actual subject becomes evidence
that the treatment exists. Signals — the product is smaller than the display type
by an order of magnitude; the photography is desaturated so the type can dominate;
the concept is legible before the thing is.

The colour equivalent is worth naming here too: composition decides **where the
colour masses sit and what they weigh**; [color-taste](../../color-taste/SKILL.md)
decides their chromatic relationships. **Colour cannot rescue an unresolved mass
structure** — a new accent on a page with five equal masses produces five equal
masses with an accent.
