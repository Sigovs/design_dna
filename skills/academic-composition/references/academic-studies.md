# Academic studies

*Read when: you cannot see what is wrong, or you want to test a composition before
building it. These are diagnostic exercises borrowed from studio practice —
**analytical tools, not deliverables.** Run the one that fits; never run all
twelve as documentation.*

Each study works the same way: **remove information until only one relationship
remains, then judge that relationship alone.** Their power is entirely in the
removal — a study that keeps the detail tells you nothing you did not already see.

---

## A · Thumbnail reduction

Reduce the full composition to ~200px wide. Judge: does the hierarchy survive, is
there a dominant, where does the eye land first?

*Catches:* absent dominant, fragmented centres, weak tonal structure. Almost every
composition failure is visible here, which is why it is first.

## B · Major-mass study

Remove all text and detail. Represent the work as 3–7 flat shapes.

*Catches:* component-first composition, uniform masses, surface fragmentation. If
you cannot produce the shapes, [C15](../SKILL.md#invariant) is already failing.

## C · Tonal study

Reduce to 3–5 values. Nothing else.

*Catches:* hierarchy carried by hue, figure-ground ambiguity, flat depth. This is
[C3](../SKILL.md#invariant) made operational.

## D · Silhouette study

Convert the major elements to solid shapes and look only at contour and area.

*Catches:* accidental sameness of mass, awkward proportion pairs, headline
silhouettes that fight the image beside them.

## E · Centre-of-gravity study

Mark one point per major mass. Look at the distribution.

*Catches:* drift, one-sided loading, local balance without global balance. See
[optical-balance](optical-balance.md).

## F · Directional-force map

Draw the vectors: image direction, gaze, perspective, alignment axes, diagonals,
scroll.

*Catches:* the eye being sent out of the page, unresolved opposing forces, a
centre of action nothing points to. See
[direction-and-movement](direction-and-movement.md).

## G · Negative-space study

Represent **only** the empty fields — invert the composition.

*Catches:* trapped space, unshaped space, gaps of identical size doing different
jobs. The most under-used study, and the one that most often explains "it feels
off".

## H · Rhythm study

Strip meaning; map only recurrence, interval, accent, pause, culmination as a
vertical strip.

*Catches:* pure meter, absent pause, premature culmination, alternation
masquerading as rhythm. See
[rhythm-interval-and-pause](rhythm-interval-and-pause.md).

## I · Edge study

Inspect only how masses meet the frame: bleeding, cropping, approaching, clearing.

*Catches:* near-bleeds, tangencies, mixed edge logic across sections.

## J · Responsive recomposition study

Reduce desktop and mobile each to masses, side by side. Compare centre, dominance,
rhythm.

*Catches:* the vanished counterweight, the stacked miniature, the first-screen
priority nobody re-decided.

## K · Section-sequence study

Represent a scrolling page as a vertical strip of events: density, scale, tonal
change, one row per section.

*Catches:* sagging middles, equal-weight sections, endings that stop rather than
resolve. Best paired with H.

## L · Contradictory-content stress test

Replace the hero image with a worse one. Triple the headline length. Double the
item count. Remove the photography entirely.

*Catches:* compositions whose mechanism depends on one asset — the single most
expensive failure to discover after launch, and the one placeholder copy hides.

## Choosing a study

| Complaint | Run |
|---|---|
| "Something's wrong, I don't know what" | A, then B |
| "It looks flat" | C |
| "It feels off but it's aligned" | E, then G |
| "The eye goes somewhere strange" | F |
| "It's endless / boring" | H, K |
| "Fine on desktop, bad on phone" | J |
| "It fell apart when real content arrived" | L |
| "Beautiful sections, weak page" | K, then B |

## Using them honestly

- A study is worth running only if you are prepared for it to say the composition
  is wrong. Running one to confirm a decision already made is decoration.
- Studies produce **observations**, which become lines in the
  [Composition Read](../SKILL.md#composition-read) — not attachments.
- Two studies disagreeing is information: usually the problem sits at a different
  level than assumed. See [diagnosis](diagnosis.md).
