# Academic foundations

*Read when: constructing a new page, or when a composition feels wrong and you
cannot yet say why. This file explains the method the other references apply.*

---

## What "academic" means here, and what it does not

It means a **method of seeing**, developed in studio practice: reduce, observe,
relate, decide, test. It does not mean a period style, a proportion system, or a
canon of approved arrangements. Nothing in this skill will make work look
classical.

The method is worth importing into interface design for one reason: it was built
to answer *"what is actually happening in this field of forces?"* before
*"what should I add?"* — and generated layout work fails in the opposite order,
adding components until the space is used.

## The four questions, in order

**1. What is here?** — observation. What forces already exist in the content, the
imagery, the format and the context, before you have made a single decision.
Long headlines are a force. A client photo library shot in one direction is a
force. Three products with wildly different silhouettes is a force. Content you
have not been given is a force, and usually the decisive one.

**2. What should this be?** — artistic intention. The world the composition
constructs: monumental or intimate, documentary or theatrical, urgent or
contemplative, static or dynamic, open or closed, immediate or progressively
revealed. Stated as observable conditions, never as adjectives —
[the Read bans the adjective list](../SKILL.md#composition-read).

**3. What are the relationships?** — the body of the method: masses, centre,
dominance, balance, direction, rhythm, space, tension, unity. Each is a
relationship, not a property of an element. *Large* is meaningless; *three times
the mass beside it, against a field twice its own area* is a decision.

**4. Does it survive?** — validation. Thumbnail, grayscale, changed content,
changed image, changed format, keyboard, and the visitor's actual task.

## Observation before intention

Observation is the stage most often skipped, and skipping it is how a brief's
real constraint arrives too late to shape anything.

Look at, and write down:

- **The real content** — lengths, quantities, the longest and shortest case, what
  repeats, what appears once, what will change weekly.
- **The real imagery** — how many, how consistent, what they are shot against,
  where their subjects face, how much crop tolerance they have. One hero image
  that works is not an image library.
- **The real task** — what the visitor came to do, in plain words, and the means
  that serve it. This becomes `TASK:` and `MEANS:` in the report and is protected
  by [C14](../SKILL.md#invariant).
- **The real format** — not "desktop and mobile" but the actual viewport
  proportions, the fixed chrome, the safe areas, whether the page is met all at
  once or as a scrolled sequence.
- **The real constraints** — CMS structure, editorial capacity, photography
  budget, load budget, the client's own conventions.

**Missing content is a finding, not a gap to be filled with lorem.** If the
composition's mechanism depends on copy or an image you do not have, say so in the
Read under *unresolved dependencies* and design a mechanism that survives without
it. An elaborate layout that only works with the placeholder hero is a layout that
does not work.

## Artistic image before layout

The artistic image is the answer to *"what world does this construct?"* — and it
is decided from the subject, not from a fashionable layout style.

Ask, and answer in observable terms:

| Question | A weak answer | A usable answer |
|---|---|---|
| What register? | "premium" | "institutional and unhurried — one event per screen, nothing competing for the same second" |
| Static or dynamic? | "dynamic" | "static overall, with a single diagonal in the third screen that the rest of the page resolves" |
| Open or closed? | "open" | "closed: every mass is contained by the format, nothing bleeds, the frame holds the subject" |
| Dense or expansive? | "clean" | "dense in the upper third where the decision is made, expansive below where the evidence is read" |
| What is perceived first, second, later? | "the hero" | "the price and condition first; the car second; the provenance only after a deliberate pause" |
| What stays quiet? | — | "everything in the lower band; it is support and must never compete with the opening mass" |

The artistic image and the structure develop **recursively**. A mass scheme that
will not carry the intended image is evidence about the image as much as about the
scheme — but the image is what gets revised deliberately, not abandoned quietly
because a grid was easier.

## Composition is not these things

Each of these is an *instrument* of composition. None is composition, and reaching
for one first is the standard failure:

| Instrument | What it cannot do |
|---|---|
| A grid | Decide what the masses are, or which one dominates |
| Alignment | Create balance — alignment is exact, balance is optical |
| Spacing consistency | Shape negative space; a consistent gap can still be a dead gap |
| Visual hierarchy (as size) | Rank forces — a bigger heading is not a dominant |
| Rule of thirds / golden ratio | Substitute for looking; both are arithmetic wearing authority |
| Cards | Group meaningfully — a card is a surface, not a relationship |
| Section variety | Produce rhythm; variety without a pattern is noise |
| Asymmetry | Create tension; asymmetry without counterweight is drift |
| Conversion patterns | Decide what the page is about |

## Diagnosing at the right altitude

Most "composition problems" reported by a client or a critic are symptoms. Before
changing the composition, locate the level:

1. **Content** — the hierarchy is unclear because the content has no hierarchy.
2. **Typography** — the masses are right but the type is set at the wrong weight
   or measure, so the texture is wrong.
3. **Imagery** — the layout is sound but the images point out of the page, or are
   too detailed to serve as a quiet field.
4. **Colour** — the tonal structure is right and the chroma is fighting it.
5. **Composition** — the masses themselves are wrong: no centre, no dominant, no
   interval, competing forces.
6. **Interaction** — the composition is fine at rest and collapses in a state
   nobody composed (open menu, error, empty, loading, long content).

Fixing at the wrong level is the most expensive error available: a new accent
colour cannot repair an absent centre, and re-cropping every image cannot repair a
page with five equal masses. Full vocabulary in [diagnosis](diagnosis.md).
